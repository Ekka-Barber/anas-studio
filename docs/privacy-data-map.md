# Privacy data map and request runbook (P06, I31)

What personal data the system holds today, why, who can read it and how long it stays, then how a request to see, correct or delete it is handled. Buyers are guests (D08). P07 stores their contact and delivery details when a checkout hold is created; P08 adds payments, delivery, returns, refunds, availability notices and the buyer's export and erase (`privacy_buyer_export` and `privacy_buyer_erase`, tested in `tests/integration/privacy-requests.test.ts`). What E08 still has to settle is the approval of the privacy text and the accounting retention period of paid orders: the table says "as E08 sets" where that period applies, and no automatic purge touches such a record today. How a payment settles is in [payments-runbook](payments-runbook.md), and the owner's screens are in [operations](operations.md).

## Handling a request

**Verify the person first.** A visitor proves they own the address by writing from it, or by answering a message sent to it. A staff member is verified by the owner. Record the request, the check and the outcome in the deletion ledger (below) when anything is deleted.

**Where to run the SQL.** In the Supabase dashboard's SQL editor, or `supabase db query --linked "<sql>"`. The erase functions have no API grants, so nothing in the app can call them.

### A contact-form visitor

- **See or export:** `select id, name, email, message, created_at from public.contacts where email = lower(btrim('<address>'));`. The query matches the address exactly as stored, and the contact function stores a non-ASCII domain as punycode (`x@مثال.السعودية` is stored as `x@xn--mgbh0fb.xn--mgberp4a5d4ar`), so convert the domain first: `node -e "console.log(new URL('http://'+process.argv[1]).hostname)" <domain>`. When unsure, search the local part instead: `... where email ilike '<local part>@xn--%';` (`ilike`, because stored addresses are lower-case and the requester may have written capitals; `_` and `%` in a local part match any one character or any run of characters, so check that every row returned is the requester's before any of its ids goes into `privacy_erase_contacts`). Zero rows for an address with a non-ASCII domain means the conversion was skipped, not that nothing is held. Send the rows back to that address. Their notices are also in the owner's mailbox.
- **Correct:** update the matching rows by `id`.
- **Delete:** list the ids with the query above (with the converted domain, so no row is missed), then `select public.privacy_erase_contacts(array['<id>', ...]::uuid[]);`. It deletes those messages and any notice still waiting to be sent for them; sent outbox rows keep only a dangling `contactId` and the staff recipient. Anas deletes the notices from his mailbox himself. The rate-limit hashes expire within 2 days. Add the call to the deletion ledger.

### A departed staff member

1. Revoke them in the team screen (built: `active = false`, and Supabase Auth bans the user). Add the revoke to the deletion ledger as a line `revoke <user id>`: a restore brings the member back active and unbanned, and the team screen has no SQL call to record.
2. If they ask for erasure: `select public.privacy_erase_staff('<user id>');`. It refuses an active member. It replaces their email in `auth.users` with `erased-<id>@erased.invalid`, clears their Auth metadata and pending address changes, deletes their identities, sessions, refresh tokens, TOTP factors, one-time tokens and the Auth log of their own actions, puts the placeholder in place of their address in the Auth log entries about them (the owner's invite, the ban) and on sent notices addressed to them, drops unsent ones, and renames their staff row «موظف سابق». The Auth user itself stays, because deleting it would rewrite `audit_events.actor`, which the append-only trigger refuses (I31). The audit history keeps their id, which no longer leads to a name or an address.
3. Add the call to the deletion ledger.

### A buyer (P07, P08)

Buyers are guests (D08): a buyer request touches customers, orders and their records only, never `auth.users`. The two functions below take the buyer's address, are safe to repeat and have no API grant at all (not even `service_role`), so the owner runs them as the migration role, as in "Where to run the SQL" above. Verify the person first (above). The address is normalized as checkout stores it (lower-case, trimmed), and an internationalized domain must be given in its punycode form, converted as for a contact-form visitor: an unconverted one is refused with `Invalid address.`.

- **See or export:** `select public.privacy_buyer_export('<address>');` returns one JSON document with `email`, `customer` (name, email, phone and times; null when there is none), `orders` (number, status, mode, times, the contact snapshot with its address, the totals and the coupon code), `items` (SKU, titles, quantity, prices, the dedication, the preorder date and note), `fulfillments` (state, carrier, tracking, times), `entitlements` (when granted or withdrawn, and whether a file is attached), `refunds` (amount, status, source, times), `returns` (items, the buyer's reason, state, times), `notifications` (SKU and titles, status, consent revision, times) and `mail` (the kind, status and times of every mail sent to the address). Orders are matched through the address's hash and the customer row. It leaves out tokens and their hashes, idempotency and request keys, checkout sessions, storage keys, Moyasar's ids and the team's own words (a return's note, a refund's reason, a dispute's text, a closed review payment's reason), which can hold personal data about the person. **It also has no payment records:** no payment attempt (amount, status, mode, the source's type and company, paid time), no review payment and no dispute of these orders, so a request answered from the document alone would miss them. For the order numbers the export lists, read the team's words that most often hold personal data, and the payment records the export lacks (these rows hold no card number, no cardholder name and no IP address); look at the order screens for the rest of the team's words:

  ```sql
  select o.order_number, q.staff_note
    from finance.return_requests q join finance.orders o on o.id = q.order_id
   where o.order_number in ('<number>', ...) and q.staff_note is not null;
  select o.order_number, r.reason
    from finance.refunds r join finance.orders o on o.id = r.order_id
   where o.order_number in ('<number>', ...);
  select o.order_number, a.status, a.amount_halalas, a.currency, a.environment,
         a.source_type, a.source_company, a.paid_at
    from finance.payment_attempts a join finance.orders o on o.id = a.order_id
   where o.order_number in ('<number>', ...) order by o.order_number, a.created_at;
  select o.order_number, v.reason, v.amount_halalas, v.provider_status, v.created_at, v.closed_at
    from finance.payment_reviews v join finance.orders o on o.id = v.order_id
   where o.order_number in ('<number>', ...) order by o.order_number, v.created_at;
  select o.order_number, d.kind, d.direction, d.amount_halalas, d.occurred_on, d.decision
    from finance.disputes d
    left join finance.payment_attempts a on a.id = d.attempt_id
    left join finance.payment_reviews v on v.provider_payment_id = d.review_payment_id
    join finance.orders o on o.id in (a.order_id, v.order_id)
   where o.order_number in ('<number>', ...) order by o.order_number, d.created_at;
  ```

  Send the document, with what the notes say about the person and the rows of the three payment queries, to that address.
- **Correct:** the customer's name and phone in «العملاء» (the owner; the email is read-only). An order keeps the contact snapshot it was placed with, because the receipt and the shipment used it: a correction there is the owner's decision and the admin does not offer it.
- **Delete:** `select public.privacy_buyer_erase('<address>');`. It locks every order of the address first, then deletes the address's notification rows and its unsent buyer mail (`receipt`, `order_link`, `order_shipped`, `order_refunded`, `order_ready`, `notify_confirm`, `availability`); replaces the address in the buyer mail that remains (the sent history) with one placeholder `erased-<uuid>@erased.invalid` per call (an owner alert or a contact notice to an address that is also a staff address stays, because it is not the buyer's); deletes the orders the retention rule allows, with their payment attempts, webhook events, items, reservations and coupon uses; and deletes the customer row when no order of it is left. It answers `{notifications, orders, outbox_dropped, outbox_redacted, customers, kept: [{orderNumber, status, reason}]}` and writes one count-only `privacy.erase_buyer` audit row with no address and no id. An order is kept, with its reason: `ACCOUNTING_RETENTION` (`paid`, `paid_needs_resolution` or `refunded`), `OPEN_HOLD` (`pending_payment`: when its 20-minute hold ends it becomes `expired`, and a second run then deletes it unless money or work is attached) or `PAYMENT_RECORDS` (an expired or cancelled order that still has money or work attached: an attempt creating, pending, uncertain, paid or in review, one marked `UNVERIFIED` or still due for a check, a review payment of any state, or a dispute). There is no age to wait for: the 90 days belong to the daily purge, not to a request.
- **The ledger line:** add the call to the deletion ledger like any other (next section). For a buyer the identifier is the address itself, because the function takes the address and a restore can only be repaired by running it again with it; the ledger is a private file outside every backup, so keep it as private as the backups. Whether the ledger may hold an erased person's address is for E08 to confirm.
- **What stays:** paid, under-review and refunded orders, with their attempts, review payments, refunds, returns, fulfilments, entitlements and disputes, under the accounting retention E08 sets (the answer to the person names the orders, the reason and, once E08 sets it, the period); no job deletes them today. Kept orders keep their contact snapshot. Moyasar keeps its own payment records and Resend its sent mail, under their own policies: the person asks them. Backups made before the erase still hold the data (below).

## The deletion ledger and restores

The ledger is a plain text file next to the backups, `ANASAQ-backups/deletion-ledger.txt` on Anas's machine, outside every backup, so restoring an older backup cannot roll it back (DATA-AND-SECURITY). Each line is a date and either the exact function call that was run or `revoke <user id>` (a team-screen revoke), identifiers only (for a buyer, the address the erase call takes, as above), never other erased content. After any restore, and before the site reopens, first re-revoke in the team screen every member a `revoke` line lists (`privacy_erase_staff` refuses an active member, and a restored older backup has them active again), then run every function-call line again: the three functions (`privacy_erase_contacts`, `privacy_erase_staff` and `privacy_buyer_erase`) are safe to repeat.

Backup files made before a deletion still hold the erased data until Anas deletes those files (D35 sets no automatic expiry). Say so in the answer to the person; do not promise erasure from every backup at once.
